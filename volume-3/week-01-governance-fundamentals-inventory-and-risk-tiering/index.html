    Week 1: Governance fundamentals, inventory and risk tiering¶

    Volume 3 · Weeks 1-2: Governance operating model and global regulation

    You will be able to: define who owns AI risk in an organisation, build an AI inventory, and tier use cases by risk so that effort goes where it matters.

    First line owns the risk, second line oversees it, third line gives independent assurance.

    Core concepts

    • Why governance exists. AI systems make or shape decisions, learn from data, and change behaviour over time. Governance is the set of roles, rules and evidence that keeps their use accountable, lawful and aligned with the organisation's risk appetite.
    • Roles. Typical roles are a board or executive sponsor, an AI governance committee (security, legal, privacy, risk, data, business), model or system owners, a risk function, and internal audit. Write down who decides, who advises and who is informed for every major decision.
    • Three lines of defence. The first line owns and operates the AI system and its controls. The second line (risk, compliance, security) sets policy and oversees. The third line (internal audit) gives independent assurance.
    • AI inventory. You cannot govern what you have not found. The inventory lists every AI system and use case: owner, purpose, model and vendor, data used, users affected, deployment status and risk tier. Include shadow AI (tools staff use without approval).
    • Use-case intake. A short, mandatory form that new AI uses go through before build or purchase. It feeds the inventory and triggers the right level of review.
    • Risk tiering. Sort use cases into tiers (for example low, medium, high, prohibited) using criteria such as impact on individuals, decision autonomy, data sensitivity, scale, reversibility and regulatory category. Higher tiers get more review, testing and monitoring.

    Study plan (about 8 hours)

    1. Read an introductory guide on three lines of defence (any reputable audit-body or risk-framework explanation), about 1.5 hours.
    2. Read the governance sections of the NIST AI Risk Management Framework (the "Govern" function) and skim ISO/IEC 42001's management-system structure, about 2.5 hours.
    3. Lab and deliverable, about 4 hours.

    Guided lab: inventory and tiering for a fictional enterprise

    1. Invent a mid-sized regulated enterprise (for example a regional insurer or logistics company) with at least 12 AI uses: some built in house, some bought, some shadow AI.
    2. Design the inventory as a spreadsheet with the columns above. Fill it in.
    3. Draft a one-page intake form that a business user can complete in ten minutes.
    4. Write a tiering rubric with five to seven criteria, each scored 1-3, and clear tier thresholds. Test it by tiering all your use cases and checking that the results feel defensible. Revise the rubric if two very different uses land in the same tier.
    5. Add your Volume 1 and Volume 2 system as one of the inventory entries, with the red-team findings as risk evidence.

    Deliverable (commit to volume3/week1/): the inventory template (populated), the intake form, and the tiering rubric.

    Self-check

    1. What does each of the three lines of defence do?
    2. Why does an inventory need to include shadow AI?
    3. Name four criteria you would use to tier an AI use case, and justify one.
    4. What should happen automatically when a use case lands in the highest tier?
    5. Who should approve exceptions to the tiering rules?
