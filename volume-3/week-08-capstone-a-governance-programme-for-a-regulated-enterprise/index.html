    Week 8: Capstone: a governance programme for a regulated enterprise¶

    Volume 3 · Weeks 7-8: Assurance, monitoring and the capstone

    You will be able to: design, justify and present an AI governance programme that a board can approve.

    The brief

    The curriculum asks you to design an AI governance programme for a regulated enterprise and present it to a simulated board. Choose one from a list that interests you or that matches your career target: a bank, an insurer, a hospital group, a government agency, an airline or a telecom operator. Give it a realistic size, geography (include at least two jurisdictions from Week 4) and a portfolio of 10 to 15 AI use cases, including at least one agentic use case.

    What to deliver

    1. Programme charter. Purpose, scope, principles, governance bodies, decision rights, and escalation paths.
    2. Operating model. Roles, three-lines responsibilities, a committee calendar, and the intake-to-retirement lifecycle for an AI use case.
    3. Inventory and tiering result. Your Week 1 artefacts applied to the chosen enterprise.
    4. Regulatory map. A summary of the obligations register, with dates, owners and confidence levels.
    5. Policy and control set. The policy stack and the control objectives it implies.
    6. Risk picture. The top ten risks with ratings, treatments and indicators.
    7. Assurance plan. What is tested, how often, and by whom.
    8. Roadmap. A phased plan over 12 to 24 months with milestones, owners, rough resource needs and dependencies. Show what happens in the first 90 days.
    9. Board presentation. Eight to ten minutes, five slides at most: why it matters, what we do, what it costs and delivers, the risks and decisions needed, the asks.

    Study plan (about 8 hours)

    1. Read two examples of board-level reports on technology or cyber risk, noting how they frame decisions, about 1.5 hours.
    2. Plan and assemble the capstone, about 6.5 hours. Expect to extend into a second week.

    Quality checks before you present

    • Every claim about a law or standard cites its source and a date.
    • Every top risk links to evidence or a clearly labelled assumption.
    • The roadmap shows trade-offs and what you are deliberately not doing.
    • Someone outside the field could say what decision you want from the board.

    Deliverable (commit to volume3/week8/): the programme pack, the roadmap, and the board presentation with speaker notes. This is your Volume 3 gate artefact.

    Self-check

    1. What decisions do you need from the board, and what happens if they say no?
    2. What are the first three things you would do in the first 90 days?
    3. Which part of your programme depends most on regulation that may change, and how will you handle that?
    4. How will you know in a year that the programme is working?
    5. What would an auditor challenge first in your pack?
