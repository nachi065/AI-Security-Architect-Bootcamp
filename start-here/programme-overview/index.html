    Programme Overview¶

    The AI Security Architect Bootcamp trains security and engineering professionals to design, test, govern and defend enterprise AI systems, including LLM applications, RAG pipelines and autonomous agents. It is organised as four volumes that follow one running case study: you build an AI system, attack it, govern it and finally re-architect it securely.

    Programme at a glance¶

    Volume Title Weeks Hours (est.) Gate output
    Volume 1 Foundations: Build It First 13 ~120–130 Foundations Gate + working RAG/agent + threat model
    Volume 2 Advanced AI Red Teaming & Adversarial Testing 8 ~70 Red-team report on a provided target
    Volume 3 AI Governance, Compliance & Risk Programs 8 ~65 Governance programme pack (policy, risk register, roadmap)
    Volume 4 Secure AI Architecture & Engineering (Architect Core + Capstone) 8 ~80 Architecture package + live design-authority defence
    Total 37 ~330–350 Hours are planning estimates

    How this relates: The table is taken from the Master Curriculum (v2.0, revised 5 October 2026). Where it mentions a provided target (Volume 2), the self-study guides ask you to build and seed your own instead. See Volume 2, Week 8.

    Graduate outcomes¶

    • Decompose any LLM, RAG, agentic or MLOps system into components, data flows and trust boundaries, and produce a defensible threat model for it.

    • Build a working RAG pipeline and tool-using agent, then attack and harden it (build first, then break).

    • Plan and execute an AI red-team engagement with repeatable tooling, evidence and a decision-grade report.

    • Map technical risks to OWASP (LLM and Agentic lists), MITRE ATLAS, NIST and ISO controls, and to regulatory obligations in the EU, GCC and India.

    • Design reference architectures for LLM gateways, RAG, agents and MCP tool ecosystems, including identity, least privilege, guardrails, logging and kill switches.

    • Run AI vendor and third-party risk evaluations (RFP, proof of concept, contract controls).

    • Stand up an AI governance operating model: inventory, risk tiering, policy stack, assurance and monitoring.

    • Communicate AI risk and architecture decisions to engineers, auditors and the board.

    Design principles¶

    Principle What it means in practice
    Build, then break, then defend, then govern Learners build a RAG app and an agent in Volume 1, attack the same systems in Volume 2, govern them in Volume 3, and re-architect them securely in Volume 4. One running case study threads through the whole programme.
    Framework-anchored, not tool-anchored Every lab maps to a named risk (OWASP LLM / Agentic, MITRE ATLAS, NIST). Tools change quickly; the risk taxonomy and architectural principles outlast them.
    Local-first labs All core labs run on a laptop with Docker and a local model server, with no paid API keys. Cloud labs are optional extensions.
    Portfolio as evidence Each week produces an artefact (diagram, repo, report, policy). The portfolio is the credential employers and clients actually inspect.
    Living curriculum A quarterly landscape review updates the framework map (see the Landscape Currency Map). AI security standards are changing monthly.
