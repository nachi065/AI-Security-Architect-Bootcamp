    Start Here¶

    This is a four-volume, 37-week self-study programme for security and engineering professionals who want to design, test, govern and defend enterprise AI systems. The method is build first, then break, then defend, then govern. You build an AI system in Volume 1 and keep returning to the same system: you attack it in Volume 2, govern it in Volume 3 and re-architect it properly in Volume 4.

    The four volumes¶

    Volume Stage Weeks Hours (est.) You finish with
    Volume 1: Foundations Build 13 ~120-130 A working RAG application, a tool-using agent, a threat model and a portfolio repository
    Volume 2: Advanced AI Red Teaming Break 8 ~70 A CI-integrated attack suite and a red-team report
    Volume 3: AI Governance, Compliance and Risk Govern 8 ~65 A governance programme pack: policy, risk register, roadmap
    Volume 4: Secure AI Architecture and Engineering Architect 8 ~80 An architecture package defended in front of a design-authority panel

    The hours are planning estimates from the curriculum, about 330-350 in total. Track your own hours over the first three weeks and adjust.

    How to work through it¶

    1. Read the volume's landing page first. It gives that volume's prerequisites, weekly rhythm and rules.
    2. Work one week at a time: learn the concepts, do the guided lab, write the deliverable, then answer the self-check questions closed-book.
    3. Commit every deliverable to a single portfolio repository, one folder per week. Later volumes reuse what you built earlier, so keep everything runnable.
    4. Finish each volume with its gate: a closed-book exam plus a practical task. The curriculum expects 80% or higher, with one retake.
    5. Tick Mark as complete at the bottom of each week and gate. The home page shows your progress, which is stored in your own browser only.

    Pick your track¶

    If you are Do this
    Security professional (GRC, SOC, pentest, cloud) Skim Weeks 1-3 of Volume 1 in one weekend, doing only the labs you cannot already do. Spend the saved time on Python (Weeks 4-5) and ML/LLM engineering (Weeks 10-13)
    Developer or ML engineer Skim Weeks 4-5 and 7. Spend extra time on Weeks 1, 6, 8 and 9 (network, API, Kubernetes and cloud security) and on threat modelling
    Beginner or career changer Do all 13 weeks in order. Add an extra week for Linux and Python if the labs feel rushed

    A quick diagnostic: if you can already explain a TLS handshake, write a Bash loop, and open a pull request without help, take the security-professional track.

    Order of the volumes¶

    • Volume 2 needs the Volume 1 Foundations Gate, or equivalent skills.
    • Volume 3 needs the Volume 1 Foundations Gate. Volume 2 is recommended but not required on the governance track. Experienced GRC professionals can run Volume 3 in parallel with Volume 4.
    • Volume 4 needs Volumes 1 to 3, or equivalent.

    What you need¶

    • A laptop with 16 GB RAM recommended, Docker, Python 3.11 or later, Git and VS Code.
    • Ollama for local models. No paid API keys are required for the core labs.
    • A local Kubernetes cluster (kind or minikube) from Volume 1, Week 8.
    • For Volume 3, only a spreadsheet, a document editor and your Git repository.

    Ground rules¶

    • Local first. Every lab runs on your own machine.
    • Authorised targets only. Practise on systems you own and on deliberately vulnerable targets that you run locally. Never test a live third-party service.
    • Keep harmful output out of your repository. Record that an attack worked and why. Do not commit harmful generated content or publish working attack payloads against real products.
    • Verify before you trust. Tool versions, commands, framework editions and regulations change. Where a page names one, check the current official source. See the Landscape Currency Map.
    • Say what you do not know. A self-check question you cannot answer is next week's study target, not a failure.
    • Volume 3 is not legal advice. Treat regulatory content as a teaching summary and read the primary legal text.

    Self-grading¶

    A question counts only if you answered it from understanding, without looking it up. Score artefacts against the rubric at the end of each volume. If you fall short at a gate, list your three weakest topics, repeat the matching labs and retake after at least a week.

    Describing your achievement¶

    The programme's certification is awarded by the cohort programme's organisers, and a self-study path cannot issue it. What you can do is hold yourself to the same gates, have a colleague or mentor review your gate work and capstone defence, and describe your achievement accurately, for example: "completed a self-study curriculum modelled on the AI Security Architect Bootcamp".
