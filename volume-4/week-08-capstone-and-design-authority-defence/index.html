    Week 8: Capstone and design-authority defence¶

    Volume 4 · Weeks 7-8: Enterprise architecture, business case and capstone

    You will be able to: integrate everything into one defensible package for a secure agentic AI platform and defend it under questioning.

    The capstone

    The curriculum calls for an integrated "Secure Agentic AI Platform" package defended before a design-authority panel. Your package combines your work from all four volumes into a single coherent story.

    Package contents

    1. Executive summary (one page): the platform, its purpose, the main risks, the recommendation.
    2. Architecture: context and component diagrams, data flows, trust boundaries, identities, deployment topology, and ADRs for the ten most important decisions.
    3. Threat model: baseline from Week 1, updated for the final design, with each threat traced to a control.
    4. Security evidence: results of your attack suite against the final design, before-and-after comparisons, guardrail measurements, and detection test results.
    5. Governance mapping: your control set mapped to the EU AI Act classification, your chosen frameworks and the regional obligations register, with dates and confidence levels.
    6. Operations: monitoring, incident playbooks, change management and model-intake process.
    7. Business case and roadmap: from Week 7, trimmed to what the panel needs.
    8. Residual risk statement: risks that remain after the design, their owners, acceptance decisions and review dates.

    Study plan (about 10 hours, plus capstone time)

    1. Assemble the package, close gaps, and resolve contradictions between earlier artefacts, about 6 hours. Expect to take a second week if needed.
    2. Prepare a 20-minute presentation and a list of likely questions, about 2 hours.
    3. Rehearse twice, once with a timer and once with a friend or colleague asking hard questions, about 2 hours.

    The defence

    The format is a 20-minute presentation followed by 20 minutes of questions. The panel, even if it is only you and one or two colleagues or mentors, is there to challenge. Prepare for questions such as these:

    • Which threat worries you most, and why have you not reduced it further?
    • What happens if the model is fully compromised? Show the blast radius.
    • How do you know your guardrails work on real traffic and not only your test set?
    • Which of your legal claims are most likely to change in a year, and how will you notice?
    • What would you do differently with half the budget? With double?
    • Which assumption, if wrong, would most damage your design?
    • Where have you relied on the model behaving well rather than on enforcement?

    Deliverable (commit to volume4/capstone/): the full package and the presentation, plus a short note recording the questions you were asked and how you answered.

    Self-check

    1. What is the single strongest piece of evidence in your package?
    2. Where is your design weakest, and have you said so openly?
    3. How would you respond if the panel rejected your top recommendation?
    4. Which earlier artefact needed correcting once you saw it with the others?
    5. What would a reviewer need to reproduce your test results?

    The eight parts of your capstone package. Every earlier weekly artefact feeds one of them.
