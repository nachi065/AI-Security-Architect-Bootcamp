    Week 4: Regional regulation: GCC and India¶

    Volume 3 · Weeks 3-4: Frameworks, standards and regional regulation

    You will be able to: build a multi-jurisdiction obligations register for a single AI use case and mark how sure you are of each entry.

    Read this first. The table below is a snapshot as of 5 October 2026 from the sources listed at the end. Items marked "verified in sources" were found in documents I opened or in multiple search results; items marked "not verified" are things I could not confirm. All of it needs checking against official texts and local counsel before use.

    Jurisdiction What I found Confidence
    India The Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and the DPDP Rules 2025 were notified on 13 November 2025 and commence in three phases. Institutional provisions (including the Data Protection Board) started then; the consent-manager framework starts on 13 November 2026; the main obligations (notice, consent, rights, security safeguards, breach handling, children's data, penalties) start 18 months after notification, on or about 13 May 2027. Sources differ by a day (12 or 13 May), so check the notification Verified in sources (law-firm summaries; read the Gazette notification)
    UAE (federal) The Personal Data Protection Law is Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021. A June 2026 law-firm note states its implementing regulations had still not been issued and that no private-sector supervisor was clearly designated. On 14 June 2026 the UAE announced a Federal Authority for Artificial Intelligence and Data to consolidate AI, data and digital-government functions. One lower-quality web source claims the regulations have since been issued; I could not corroborate that Partly verified: re-check whether implementing regulations exist now
    UAE free zones DIFC and ADGM have their own data-protection regimes and active regulators, independent of the federal law. DIFC has additional rules on autonomous and semi-autonomous systems (I recall a "Regulation 10"; verify) Partly verified
    Saudi Arabia The Personal Data Protection Law (Royal Decree M/19) came into force on 14 September 2023, with enforcement after a one-year transition from mid-September 2024, with implementing and transfer regulations. SDAIA's AI Ethics Principles (September 2023) and Generative AI Guidelines (January 2024) are soft law. Sources I saw say there is no dedicated AI law yet, though a draft Global AI Hub Law was reported in April 2025 and a dedicated AI law is widely expected. Reports also describe an SDAIA AI Adoption Framework for public bodies (late 2025) Mostly from search summaries; verify
    Oman A personal-data-protection law exists Not verified in this revision: confirm the current law, executive regulations and effective dates

    India DPDP phased commencement as of the build date. Sources differ by a day on the May 2027 date.

    Core concepts

    • Personal data law is the practical AI law in this region. Where no AI-specific statute exists, training, fine-tuning and using a model on personal data falls under data-protection duties: lawful basis or consent, purpose limitation, minimisation, security, cross-border transfer conditions, data-subject rights and breach duties.
    • Soft law still matters. Ethics principles and government frameworks are often written into tenders, procurement and sector regulator expectations, so they behave like requirements in practice.
    • Cross-border data and cloud AI. Sending prompts or documents to a model hosted abroad can be a cross-border transfer. Map where inference, logging and vendor support happen.
    • Sector regulators add duties. Financial regulators, health authorities and critical-infrastructure authorities issue their own AI and cyber rules. Add them if the use case is in a regulated sector.
    • Uncertainty is a finding. In a register, "status unclear, owner to confirm by date" is a legitimate and honest entry.

    Study plan (about 8 hours)

    1. Read the official text of the DPDP Act and Rules, focusing on obligations of data fiduciaries and the phasing, about 2.5 hours.
    2. Read the UAE and Saudi data-protection laws at article-heading level, plus SDAIA's AI Ethics Principles, about 3 hours.
    3. Lab and deliverable, about 2.5 hours.

    Guided lab: an obligations register

    1. Pick one AI use case: a customer-support assistant that uses retrieval over customer records, serving customers in the UAE, Saudi Arabia, India and the EU, hosted by a cloud provider outside those regions.
    2. For each jurisdiction, list every obligation you can find that touches the use case: consent or lawful basis, transparency, rights, security, breach reporting, transfer rules, AI-specific duties, sector rules.
    3. For each row record: obligation, source (law and article), applies from (date), status (in force, phased, draft, unclear), the control that satisfies it (from your Week 3 control set), the owner, and a confidence rating.
    4. Mark every "unclear" and write the question for counsel.
    5. Highlight overlaps. One control, such as breach notification, may satisfy several rows.

    Deliverable (commit to volume3/week4/): the obligations register with at least 25 rows, an "open questions for counsel" list, and a one-page summary of overlaps and the strictest common standard.

    Self-check

    1. Why is personal-data law central to AI compliance in jurisdictions without an AI statute?
    2. What are India's three DPDP commencement stages?
    3. Why can a prompt sent to an offshore model be a regulated transfer?
    4. Why must you record the date and source for every legal claim?
    5. When is "unclear" the right answer in a register?
